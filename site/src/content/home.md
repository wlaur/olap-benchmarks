## October 2026 · M4 Pro

**DuckDB** has the lowest median on all 18 [telemetry queries at both SF1 and SF10](#/explorer/device_telemetry?mode=database&system=macbook-m4-pro&scale=10&databases=duckdb,clickhouse,postgres,timescaledb). These are warm reads over static data; they measure neither freshness nor late-write cost.

**Polars** leads [common-Parquet SF10](#/explorer/parquet_analytics?mode=database&system=macbook-m4-pro&scale=10&databases=polars,duckdb,clickhouse) by geometric mean query ratio, 1.104 against DuckDB’s 1.191. **DuckDB** has the smaller sum of query medians, 3,376.7 ms against Polars’ 3,688.4 ms. The order reverses because the geometric score weights queries equally in log space, while the sum gives expensive queries more influence. Neither number is elapsed run time.

The new JSON representation suite separates [raw extraction](#/explorer/json_representation?mode=database&system=macbook-m4-pro&scale=10&family=json_representation_raw) from [prepared fields](#/explorer/json_representation?mode=database&system=macbook-m4-pro&scale=10&family=json_representation_prepared). Across five queries, summed medians fall from 4,012.3 to 203.9 ms for DuckDB and from 7,251.7 to 570.6 ms for ClickHouse. Field materialization costs 1.529 s and 3.242 s respectively; whole population costs are 29.909 s and 21.096 s, including repair and the other [documented source phases](https://github.com/wlaur/olap-benchmarks/blob/main/docs/campaigns/2026-10-followup.md#new-suite-measurements). Query sums exclude preparation and are not elapsed run times. These tracks carry zero overall weight.

All five participants are eligible on the 113-query [Join Order Benchmark](#/explorer/join_order?mode=database&system=macbook-m4-pro&scale=1), with matching peer hashes. PostgreSQL’s 23 upstream foreign-key indexes enabled complete coverage, but Q17a and Q26c regressed. Changed ANALYZE statistics confound the before/after controls; [the tuning evidence](https://github.com/wlaur/olap-benchmarks/blob/main/docs/campaigns/2026-10-followup.md#bounded-tuning) supports a coverage improvement without guaranteeing lower latency.

Exploratory TPC-H SF10 streams complete 15.598/21.616/21.542 requests per second for DuckDB at 1/4/16 clients, 5.587/6.366/6.243 for Polars and 4.155/4.792/4.898 for ClickHouse. PostgreSQL failed warmup at all three client counts, so its QPS stays Unknown. These are single windows with a full query manifest and fixed shared budgets; achieved query mixes differ, and there are no confidence intervals. The analytical evidence above remains separate from SELECT rankings.

## How to read these results

Fixed SELECT workloads run each query once to warm up, then three times for measurement; we report the median, including driver, transfer and conversion time. These runs are serial with warm OS caches, and [documented layouts and query tuning](https://github.com/wlaur/olap-benchmarks/blob/main/docs/campaigns/2026-10-tuning-protocol.md) are allowed.

Performance rankings require every query in a fixed comparison set. Incomplete results remain visible with coverage and execution outcomes, without a synthetic latency penalty. The comparison policy is a retrospective analysis of the October measurements; its query sets and scales are frozen to the published asset. RTABench’s 31 raw queries and 10 prepared aggregates are separate tracks in the same family. ClickBench Q28 remains in coverage but is excluded from comparable performance because its regex answers differ by engine.

Successful execution does not establish independently verified correctness. Result details distinguish peer-hash agreement, repeat-only evidence, hash limits and documented comparison exclusions. Preparation, storage and sampled memory are separate evidence; differing enforcement scopes do not support a general memory-efficiency ranking. The [follow-up report](https://github.com/wlaur/olap-benchmarks/blob/main/docs/campaigns/2026-10-followup.md) records adopted tuning, replacements and limitations, and the [catalog](#/catalog) shows suite coverage. Airbnb remains a smoke workload. TPC-derived results are not comparable to published TPC benchmarks.
